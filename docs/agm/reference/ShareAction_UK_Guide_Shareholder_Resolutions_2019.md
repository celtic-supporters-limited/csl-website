## Foreword (p.1)

ShareAction's involvement with shareholder resolutions stretches to 2009, when it (then FairPensions) was
approached by investors and environmental NGOs to co-ordinate the drafting and filing of shareholder
resolutions at the 2010 AGMs of BP and Shell. Those resolutions succeeded in giving profile within the
investment community to oil sands extraction and led to significant new public disclosures at both firms.

## Overview (p.2-3)

This guide sets out the steps organisations must take to satisfy the legal and technical requirements to file a
shareholder resolution in the UK under the Companies Act 2006, plus advice on drafting the resolution and
supporting statement, seeking co-filers, building investor support, and overcoming the associated challenges.

## Introduction (p.4-5)

> "In the simplest terms, a shareholder resolution is a proposal submitted by the shareholders of a company to
> be voted on at the annual general meeting (AGM), directing the board to take some form of action."

UK listed companies must hold an AGM annually. The board proposes the overwhelming majority of resolutions
put to shareholders, but shareholders can take the initiative and add a resolution to be put to the vote of all
shareholders with voting rights. This is "filing a shareholder resolution." The procedures are set out in the
Companies Act 2006.

Benefits of filing: it puts the issue on the agenda (voted on by all shareholders with voting rights; circulated
with a supporting statement; the company must formally respond); it attracts media attention; and it prompts
company action by creating a forcing event ahead of the AGM.

## Case Studies (p.6-8)

Four case studies are given: climate disclosure at BP and Shell (2015, the "Aiming for A" coalition, passed with
99% support after gaining management backing); corporate lobbying at BHP Billiton (2017, ACCR, did not pass
but produced a public audit of lobbying-position differences); and human rights risk reporting at First Quantum
Minerals and Linamar (2018, SHARE, both resolutions withdrawn after the companies committed to improved
reporting without a vote). None of these bears directly on CSL's own drafting decisions; noted for completeness
of the source document only.

## Legal requirements (p.9-11)

> Section 338 of the Companies Act 2006 provides that members (i.e. shareholders whose names are entered on
> the register of shareholders) may require the company to give notice of a resolution to be considered at the
> next AGM if one of the following requirements are fulfilled:

**Route 1 to filing a resolution.** The concerned members represent not less than 5% of the total voting
rights of the members entitled to vote on a shareholder resolution.

**Route 2 to filing a resolution.** The concerned members number at least 100, holding shares on which
there has been paid an average sum per shareholder of not less than £100.

"NGOs and unions are likely to rely on the second route, i.e. 100 members with shares having an aggregate
value of at least £10,000 in nominal value. The nominal value of a company's shares is different to the market
value, and will be stated in the company's Articles of Association." This is CSL's route - CSL needs 100
qualifying shareholders, which is why the admin page's count reads "of 100 needed to lodge."

Where all 100 persons wishing to file are registered shareholders, each should complete Appendix 1.

### Indirect investors

Most of the 100 are unlikely to be registered shareholders; many will be "indirect investors" holding through
nominees such as stockbrokers. Section 153 of the Companies Act 2006 lets indirect investors be included
within the 100 once certain conditions are met: the shares must be held by a registered shareholder on their
behalf in the course of a business, and the indirect investor must have the right to instruct that shareholder how
to exercise the voting rights. Indirect investors should complete Appendix 2, accompanied by a letter on the
headed notepaper of the registered shareholder holding the shares on their behalf.

Where the resolution filers include both registered shareholders and indirect investors, section 153 applies, and
registered shareholders in that mixed group should complete Appendix 3 rather than Appendix 1.

### Conditions for a shareholder resolution

A company is not required to give notice of the resolution or circulate the supporting statement unless:

1. The resolution must not be ineffective if passed (e.g. it cannot direct the company to do something unlawful
   or inconsistent with the company's articles of association); frivolous or vexatious; or defamatory of any
   person.
2. The resolution and supporting statement must be received by the company at least six weeks before the
   AGM, or before the company gives notice of the AGM to its members.

If CSL wants the company to bear the costs of circulation, the Companies Act 2006 requires the resolution and
supporting statement to be received before the end of the financial year preceding the AGM (some companies
specify a later date - the guide gives BP's example of 21 January). It is advisable to contact the company
secretary's office early to confirm the applicable requirements, deadlines and preferred paperwork format, and
to seek formal legal advice on the proposed resolution.

## Filing a shareholder resolution (p.12-13)

A resolution must direct the board to take a particular action. This is a special resolution requiring 75% of
votes cast to pass. For institutional investor support it is advisable that the resolution focus on one issue, be
achievable in a reasonable timeframe, not be overly burdensome or costly, and be based on protecting
shareholder value over the long term.

**Supporting statement.** Under section 314 of the Companies Act 2006, resolution filers can request the
company circulate to all shareholders a supporting statement prepared by the filers, not exceeding 1,000
words, covering why the resolution should be supported, financial implications, and independent references.

**Practical challenges named by the guide:** securing the 100 separate shareholders needed (may require
individuals to purchase certificated shares through an execution-only broker to be added to the register);
knowing which paperwork applies given how varied share-holding arrangements are; and it being preferable,
though not required, to have institutional shareholders among the co-filing group for credibility, even though it
is legally possible to file with 99 individuals holding a single share each plus one institutional holder meeting
the nominal capital threshold.

**Key steps to filing**, as boxed by the guide: start early (paperwork and co-filers can take several months; the
deadline may be as much as six months before the AGM); review the company's articles of association for
resolution requirements and to confirm nominal share value; contact the company secretary as early as possible;
word the resolution as a direction to the board requesting action; secure 100 individual shareholders with an
aggregate nominal value of £10,000; and use the correct paperwork for the nature of each shareholding.

## Towards the annual general meeting (p.14-15)

Covers engaging with investors after filing, investor briefings, coordinating with other campaigns facing similar

## Appendix 1 - Requisition for shareholder resolution: Registered shareholders (p.17)

> To be completed by a registered shareholder where all 100 resolution filers are registered shareholders. Where
> the resolution filers include both registered shareholders and indirect investors, registered shareholders should
> complete a requisition in the form set out in Appendix 3.

```
                                                      [Name of Company]
                                REQUISITION FOR SHAREHOLDER RESOLUTION

 Name of member

 Address

In accordance with sections 314 and 338 of The Companies Act 2006, I/we hereby request
[ Name of Company ] p.l.c to give to members of the company entitled to receive notice of
the [ year ] Annual General Meeting of [ Name of Company ] p.l.c, notice of the attached
resolution and the text of the supporting statement (which I/we have signed for the purposes
of identification), which resolution is a resolution that may properly be moved and is
intended to be moved at that meeting.

I/we confirm that:

1. I/we am/are [a] member[s] of the company (that is the person whose name appears on
     [ Name of Company ] p.l.c's Register of Members);

2. I/we hold [ number of ] Shares in account number (URN): [ account number ];
3. The total amount paid up on the shares is: [ amount ];
4. I/we do not hold these shares on behalf of someone else;
5. All these shares confer voting rights that are relevant for the purposes of making requests
     under sections 314 and 338 of the Companies Act 2006.

Signature  Date
```

**This is the form CSL's own requisition tracks.** Every direct registered shareholder signing through
`/resolution` is completing the substance of this form: name, address, share account reference (mapped to
`computershare_srn`), share class, and the same five-part confirmation set, adapted to CSL's own
`eligibility_confirmed` / `resolution_supported` / `consent_given` fields. The declaration frame - "in accordance
with sections 314 and 338 ... notice of the attached resolution and the text of the supporting statement" - is
what `docs/agm/CSL_AGM_AdminRedesign_ClaudeCode_Prompt.md` section 3a required CSL's own `declaration_text`
to be brought into line with; CSL's original wording cited section 338 alone and did not mention the supporting
statement.

**Two fields this form asks for that CSL does not currently capture:** the amount paid up on the shares
(confirmation 3), and an explicit "I/we do not hold these shares on behalf of someone else" confirmation
(confirmation 4) distinct from CSL's own eligibility confirmation. Both are open items awaiting the solicitor, per
the AdminRedesign brief - not added speculatively here.

## Appendix 2 - Requisition for shareholder resolution: Indirect investors (p.18)

> To be completed by an indirect investor who holds shares through a nominee. It must be accompanied by
> written confirmation on the nominee's headed notepaper.

Cites section 153 rather than sections 314/338 directly in its opening request sentence, and requires a
"Statement made pursuant to section 153(2)" naming the registered shareholder ("the member") who holds the
shares on the indirect investor's behalf, that member's account number, the number and paid-up amount of
shares held on the investor's behalf, confirmation the shares are not held on behalf of anyone else, that some or
all confer relevant voting rights, and that the investor has the right to instruct the member how to vote.

**Relevant to CSL's nominee/platform path**, `how_held: "nominee"` in `agm_signatures`. CSL's current nominee
flow does not collect a section 153(2) statement or a nominee's headed-notepaper confirmation - flagged as an
open item for the solicitor, not implemented here.

## Appendix 3 - Requisition for shareholder resolution: Registered shareholders and indirect investors (p.19)

> To be completed by a registered shareholder where the 100 resolution filers include both registered
> shareholders and indirect investors.

Same confirmation structure as Appendix 1, but its opening request sentence cites section 153 rather than
sections 314 and 338 directly, reflecting that the filing group as a whole relies on section 153 to combine
registered and indirect holders even though this particular signatory is a registered member.
